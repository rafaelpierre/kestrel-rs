    The sky appears blue because Rayleigh scattering causes shorter wavelengths of sunlight, like blue, to scatter more than longer wavelengths as they pass through the atmosphere.

    How Rayleigh Scattering Works

    Sunlight contains all visible colors, from violet (~380 nm) to red (~700 nm). When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it encounters gas molecules such as nitrogen and oxygen, which are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light. The oscillating electric field of the light wave induces motion in the electrons of these molecules, turning them into tiny radiating dipoles that scatter light in all directions scienceinsights.org+2. This scattering is elastic, meaning the light’s energy (and color) is preserved, but its direction is changed Science Notes and Projects.

    Wavelength Dependence

    The intensity of Rayleigh scattering is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the wavelength (I ∝ 1/λ⁴). This means shorter wavelengths scatter far more efficiently than longer ones. Blue light (~450 nm) is scattered about 4–6 times more than red light (~650 nm), while violet light scatters even more. However, the sky appears blue rather than violet because the Sun emits less violet light, the upper atmosphere absorbs some violet, and human eyes are more sensitive to blue scienceinsights.org+2.

    Why the Sky Changes Color

    • Daytime Sky: Blue light is scattered in all directions, so no matter where you look, you see blue light coming from the sky scienceinsights.org+1.
    • Sunsets and Sunrises: Sunlight travels through a longer path in the atmosphere, scattering away most blue and violet light. The remaining longer wavelengths, red and orange, dominate, giving the sky its warm hues scienceinsights.org+2.
    • Near the Horizon: The sky appears paler because light passes through more air, scattering blue light multiple times and mixing it with other wavelengths vhtc.org+1.

    Rayleigh vs. Mie Scattering

    Rayleigh scattering occurs when particles are much smaller than the wavelength of light, like air molecules. Larger particles, such as water droplets, dust, or pollution, scatter all wavelengths more equally, a process called Mie scattering. This is why clouds appear white or gray rather than blue scienceinsights.org+2.

    Additional Effects

    Rayleigh scattering also polarizes light, especially at 90° from the Sun, which can be observed using polarized sunglasses. This principle is used in atmospheric studies, lidar measurements, and optical technologies scienceinsights.org+1.
    In summary, the blue color of the sky is a combination of the wavelength-dependent scattering of sunlight by tiny atmospheric molecules and the human eye’s sensitivity to blue light, with Rayleigh scattering being the dominant mechanism responsible for this everyday phenomenon Wikipedia+2.
